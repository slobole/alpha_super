# Beyond 60/40 — robustness research and strategy decision (2026-11-05)

Research-only. No capital, no LIVE route. Scripts, tables and charts:
`scripts/research/beyond_6040_20261105/` (frozen spec in `SPEC.md`, outputs under `out/`).

## Verdict in one paragraph

The author's rule (B0: inverse-volatility stocks / long Treasury / gold, 8% volatility target)
is a good drawdown machine but not a Sharpe machine: over 1970–2022, measured over cash, it ties
60/40 (0.43 vs 0.45) while cutting the worst loss from −36% to −16%. Adding the author's own
per-asset trend tilt (T1: weight × 1 when the asset is above its 10-month average, × 0.5 below,
set monthly) turns it into a strategy that beats 60/40 over cash by 0.20 on the full history
(0.645 vs 0.446, bootstrap 90% band of the gap −0.02 … +0.41), halves the worst loss again
(−10.7%), and does it across a flat parameter plateau with a walk-forward test equal to the
hindsight best. The vault (2023-01 → 2026-10, opened once) landed at the 42nd percentile of the
pre-registered band. Nothing else we tried improves the design: not world stocks, not 10-year
bonds, not commodities or TIPS, not ERC/MinVar weights, not a better vol estimator, not
leverage, not a VIX hedge. Inside the house, T1 is the same engine as CORE5 with a 50-year
record; CORE5 keeps the defensive seat on 2008–2022 numbers, and T1 is registered as the
long-history validation of that engine family and as CORE5's pre-validated successor.

## The chosen rule (T1)

1. Last session of each month: w_i = (1/σ_i) / Σ(1/σ_j), σ = 63-day realised volatility, assets US stocks (VTI/SPY), 20+ year Treasury (TLT), gold (GLD).
2. Same date: m_i = 1 if total-return price > 10-month SMA, else 0; weight_i = w_i × (0.5 + 0.5·m_i).
3. Daily: if 63-day realised vol of the base (untilted) sleeve > 8.5%, exposure = 8% / vol, else 1. Final weight = exposure × weight_i. Remainder in cash (BIL).
4. No leverage, no shorts. Execution band 5% on exposure changes is free (turnover −20%, Sharpe unchanged).

Average weights 1970–2022: stocks 31%, long bond 41%, gold 29% of the risky sleeve; 64% average exposure.

## Caveats on record

- **Sharpe over cash is the honest figure.** The author's 1.0–1.2 Sharpe ignores the risk-free rate; T1 earns 3.7% a year over cash on 6% volatility. At today's 4% cash that is a 7–8% nominal return, not 12%.
- **Insurance has a premium.** In the 152 of 178 months 2008–2022 when stocks and bonds did not fall together, T1 trailed B0 by 0.25% a month; in the vault (a three-asset bull) B0 made 11.2% a year and T1 7.9%. The tilt pays in 1973–74, 1980, 2008 (Oct: −0.2% vs B0 −6.5%), 2022 (−6.3% vs −12.1% vs 60/40 −23.3%).
- **Gold did the heavy lifting pre-1985.** Gold was 38% of the 58-year dollars (author) and its 150-year real return is ~1%. The 1970s block leans on one gold bull market; T1's 1980s and 1990s blocks (gold bear) are the fair test, and T1 beats B0 there by stepping out of gold.
- **Synthetic history before 2004-11** (French market, FRED-priced 25y par bond, Frankfurt/XAU gold). Splices audited in PAKAL; ETF-era figures stand on real prices.
- **Same engine as CORE5.** Stocks / long bond / gold, risk-weighted, SMA10 on/off, vol control. CORE5 (SPY/IEF/GLD/DBC/UUP, 2007→) wins 2012→ by 0.37 Sharpe on its own era; T1 has the 1970s, 1987, 1994 and 2022 that CORE5 cannot be tested on. One pod per engine: CORE5 keeps the seat; T1 is the family's long-history validation and the pre-validated successor if CORE5 fails a review.
- **ILS reporting.** Hedge the NAV (full or 50%); do not use the trend-timed hedge on USD/ILS.
- **Turnover 2.5×/year** at 5 bp = 0.12%/yr; at 10 bp Sharpe 0.62.
