# Beyond 60/40 — robustness research and strategy decision (2026-11-05, audited)

Research-only. No capital, no LIVE route. Scripts, tables and charts:
`scripts/research/beyond_6040_20261105/` (frozen spec in `SPEC.md`, outputs under `out/`).

This record was rewritten after two independent audits on 2026-11-05 (claims vs outputs, and engine/data/statistics).
What changed and why is listed in [Audit corrections](#audit-corrections). Numbers below are the audited ones.

## Verdict

- **The author's rule (B0)** — inverse-volatility US stocks / long Treasury / gold, 8% volatility target — is a
  drawdown machine, not a Sharpe machine. Over 1970–2022, measured over cash, it ties 60/40 (0.43 vs 0.45) and cuts
  the worst loss from −36% to −16%.
- **T1** adds the author's own per-asset trend filter: each month an asset below its 10-month average goes to cash
  (its weight is 0), above it keeps its full weight. With idle cash earning a money-market rate it beats 60/40 over
  cash: **0.60–0.65 vs 0.45**, worst loss −11% to −12% vs −36%.
- **T1's edge needs the cash to earn interest.** It holds about 36% in cash on average. With cash at 0% its Sharpe
  over Fed funds is 0.30, below 60/40. Run it with the idle part in T-bills (BIL), as the house module does.
- **T1 is a sound defensive strategy, but it did not clear the strictest bar we set in advance** (beat 60/40 in at least 5 of 6 periods and never trail it by more than 0.10). It passes the full-history return gate (just),
  the drawdown gate, the plateau and the walk-forward, and the vault is consistent; it fails the per-decade clauses
  (it loses to 60/40 in the 1990s and 2010s bull markets) and the 0%-cash gate. Its tilt specification was chosen
  from a grid added after the first results, so it is the best member of a post-hoc family, not a pre-registered rule.
- **Nothing else tried changes the picture by more than noise.** A few variants pass the adoption rule on 1970–2022
  by ≤ 0.03 Sharpe (21/126/252-day vol windows, a 50/50 inverse-vol/equal blend, a 25y/10y bond mix, capped
  leverage on T1); none is adopted, and the reason (a weaker 2005–2022) is a test that was not in the spec.
- **T1 is not the same engine as CORE5** (an earlier version of this record said it was). CORE5 is five fixed 20%
  sleeves (SPY, IEF, GLD, DBC, UUP), each switched to BIL by an adaptive-average trend rule, with a small DBC short; no
  inverse-volatility weights and no portfolio volatility target. Monthly correlation 0.61. So "one pod per engine"
  does not exclude T1; whether it earns a book slot is a book question (see A12): on 2008–2022 it neither improves nor
  hurts the Defensive book, and on 2012→ it lowers its Sharpe.

## The rule (T1)

1. Last session of each month: base weight w_i = (1/σ_i) / Σ(1/σ_j), σ = 63-day realised volatility of daily price
   returns. Assets: US stocks (VTI; SPY in research), 20+ year Treasury (TLT), gold (GLD).
2. Same date: an asset whose total-return price is below its 210-session (≈10-month) average gets weight 0; above,
   weight w_i. (Formula: m = 0.5 + 0.5·s, s = ±1, so m ∈ {0, 1}.)
3. Daily: if 63-day realised vol of the untilted base sleeve > 8.5%, exposure = 8% / vol, else 1. Final weight =
   exposure × w_i × m_i. Everything else in cash — in the house module, BIL.
4. No leverage, no shorts, no band in the tested rule.

Average held mix 1970–2022 ≈ 35% stocks / 42% bond / 22% gold of the risky part; 64% average exposure.

## Caveats on record

- **Cash convention decides the edge.** Sharpe over cash: 0.65 (cash at Fed funds), 0.61 (T-bills), 0.30 (0%).
  The author's 1.0–1.2 Sharpe ignores the risk-free rate entirely.
- **Insurance has a premium.** In 152 of 178 months 2008–2022 (stocks and bonds not falling together) T1 trailed B0
  by 0.24% a month; in the vault B0 made 11.2% a year, T1 7.9%. The filter pays in 1973–74, 1980, 2008 (Oct: −0.2%
  vs B0 −6.5%) and 2022 (−6.3% vs B0 −12.1% vs 60/40 −23.3%).
- **Post-hoc selection.** SMA10 / out-below was picked from a grid added after the first results; every long-only
  cell of that grid passes, so the choice is not fragile, but it is a selection.
- **Gold did the heavy lifting before 1985.** Its 150-year real return is ~1%; the 1970s block leans on one gold bull.
- **Synthetic history before 2004-11** (French market, FRED-priced 25y par bond, Frankfurt/XAU gold), audited in PAKAL.
- **ILS reporting** (from B0): hedge the NAV (full or 50%); not the trend-timed hedge.
- **Execution:** daily re-sizing, ~226 trading days a year in the house module; a 5% band cuts T1's turnover by only 9%.
